current questions

The questions below reflect some of my current interests. My previous work has given me experience in both theoretical and empirical research, and I am interested in exploring these questions from either perspective. I would be glad to hear from researchers who find any of these ideas worth developing further.

1. Understanding Humans as a Learnable Object

  • AI has developed increasingly general models of language, images, and the physical world, while its understanding of people remains fragmented across particular tasks and interactions. Can it develop a general and transferable understanding of humans themselves?
  • We should not decide in advance what form such understanding must take. Its test is whether what the system learns continues to support prediction and explanation across unfamiliar people and situations.
  • People are not static objects of prediction: interaction with AI can change what they believe, value, can do, and choose. A trustworthy system should understand not only people as they are, but also how its own actions may shape them over time.

2. Beyond Human Preference

I am interested in how AI systems should be evaluated when human judgment is no longer a sufficient universal measure of capability.

3. Learning Across Three Timescales

I am interested in learning across ordinary, continual, and meta-learning timescales, and in what these levels may reveal about the efficiency gap between human and machine learning.

In the longer term, I hope to learn how to connect empirical research, theory, and method design more closely, so that observations can inform theory and theoretical understanding can eventually lead back to better methods.

I welcome conversations with researchers interested in developing these questions further. I plan to apply for PhD programs beginning in Fall 2028 and would be glad to hear from potential advisors who see a broader intellectual fit with my work.
